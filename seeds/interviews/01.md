The diagnosis came in March. He's four. We had a pretty good evaluator — won't name them here — but the part nobody prepared us for was what comes next. The evaluation team gives you a stack of recommendations and a list of providers and that's basically it.

We ended up at Crosswind ABA Clinic. They were one of the names on the list and they had availability sooner than the others. Honestly we had no idea how to evaluate one ABA provider against another. I read the Henderson EIBI meta-analysis twice and the paper told me ABA can work but not which providers do it well. That's the gap I keep tripping over — the research is at the population level but the choice is at the provider level.

What I can say after three months at Crosswind: they do good work on functional communication and there's a clear curriculum. Our son started using PECS within the first month. Session intensity is 20 hours a week, which felt like a lot at first; we adjusted. What I struggle with is the data they send home — it's heavy on graphs and short on context. I don't always know what to do with the information.

A friend of mine had a different experience. She got connected through Hudson Therapy Placement — the referral service — and they steered her to a smaller in-home ABA setup with a different vibe. I think Hudson's model of actual matchmaking is the right idea; we wish we'd known about them before we picked.

The other thing I wish someone had said: when you're choosing an ABA provider, ask if their BCBA does direct sessions or only supervises from a distance. Ask how often the behavior plan gets updated. Ask what the protocol is when your child has a hard day. Those are the questions that would have saved us six weeks of wondering. What I keep coming back to as a question is: how do I actually evaluate the quality of an ABA provider before committing? I never figured out a framework and I'd love one.

We're also looking at preschool placement for the fall. Spectrum Foundations Preschool came up in our parent group — a small inclusion-model preschool in our neighborhood, maybe 12 kids per class, with an embedded SLP. They have a waitlist but no formal evaluation requirement to join. We're touring next week.
