Sophia was diagnosed at 14, which I now know is late but at the time felt like a relief. Her pediatrician had been telling us for years she was "just anxious" or "shy." We finally took her to the Lighthouse Center for Autism Evaluation in White Plains and the team there — three appointments over two months — gave her a thorough workup. The lead psychologist had us read the Vasquez 2017 paper on masking and camouflaging in adolescent girls before the feedback session, and that paper essentially explained our daughter to us. I was crying while reading it.

Some things I've learned since:

The high-masking presentation in teen girls is genuinely missed by clinicians who came up before about 2015. We went through three providers before Lighthouse who variously diagnosed Sophia with social anxiety, with selective mutism, with "school refusal." None of them landed it. What Lighthouse did differently, in my opinion, was that they took a deep developmental history going back to age 2 and asked specific questions about masking behavior at school. They were already looking for it. So when other parents ask me how to find a good evaluator for a teen girl who masks — and that question comes up all the time in our parent group — my honest filter is: providers who light up when you reference the Vasquez paper are usually well-versed; providers who haven't heard of it tend to be the ones who'll miss your daughter.

For social skills, we tried Riverview Social Skills Clinic for about a year. Mixed feelings — the curriculum is solid and Sophia liked her group, but the group composition matters enormously and we got placed into a group where she was the only girl in a sea of boys. She tolerated it but didn't gain much. Riverview now offers gender-specific groups, which I gather wasn't true when we started, so others may have a different experience.

We've also tried a 14-week social cohort program through Aspire NYC, which is a smaller outfit that runs explicitly girl-only social cohorts. That experience was significantly better for Sophia. The girls in her group already had shared vocabulary about masking and that changed the dynamic.

The other resource we've found valuable is the PEERS Social Skills Curriculum — a structured social skills program developed at UCLA. We've been using a community-based provider who delivers it as a 16-week program. PEERS is built around teen-specific scenarios — dating, friendship, peer rejection — and is one of the few curricula that takes teens seriously as social actors.
